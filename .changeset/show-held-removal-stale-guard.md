A non-keyed `<Show>` or `<Match>` branch whose removal an action holds no longer throws the stale-read error when a child re-runs (#4137). The engine keeps that branch on screen as a zombie until the landing's commit disposes it, and re-runs its computations for live writes meanwhile; the narrowed accessor's guard judged the branch by the held selection (`<Show>`'s selecting read, `<Match>`'s untracked `switchFunc`), so a child memo reading the accessor during such a re-run threw "Attempting to access a stale value" and halted reactivity (`REACTIVITY_HALTED`). The guard now follows the branch's own lifetime: a cleanup on a transparent owner created right after the child marks the accessor stale when the branch's frame is disposed, which is the commit that replaces it, or its owner's death. That owner sits at the head of the frame's child chain, so it is disposed before the child's own nodes and cleanups: a read from a cleanup of the removed branch still throws, as it did before, and so does a genuine stale read after the branch is gone.
